Warmth down to 0K

Go from ordinary white through candlelight to a screen that is only red. Awning calls that end 0K. It's a name, not a real temperature: at 0K only the red part of each pixel is left.

Dimmer than the keys allow

Brightness goes from 100% down to 10%, past the lowest setting of the brightness keys, on every connected display at once.

Screenshots stay normal

The colour change happens in the display's own colour tables, so screenshots, screen recordings and screen shares keep their real colours. We checked with screenshots, QuickTime and Zoom.

On a schedule

Let Awning warm up in the evening and switch itself off in the morning. Sunset is worked out on your Mac from your city, or from your location if you allow it. Or pick your own time for each change.

Keyboard shortcuts

Turn Awning on and off with ⌃⌥⌘Z, and set your own keys for the presets, warmth and brightness.

Starts with your Mac

Awning sits in the menu bar, not the Dock, and can open at login so your evening setting is ready when you are.

Why day, evening and night

Studies show that what matters in the evening is how much blue-green light reaches your eyes. Warming the screen removes only about half of it, so Awning's evening and night presets dim the screen too. During the day there's no reason to warm your screen at all. The research also has limits: it hasn't shown that warm screens improve sleep in everyday life, or that they reduce eye strain.

Dimming without the flicker

Many screens dim by switching their backlight on and off hundreds or thousands of times a second. You can't see it directly, but some people notice it as flicker or tired eyes.

Flicker-free mode works on built-in Mac screens and Apple displays, and experimentally on one external monitor that supports DDC/CI. Some panels still pulse even at full brightness, so it can't promise zero flicker on every screen. Holding the backlight at 100% uses a little more battery.

Awning doesn't go online.

No account, no analytics, no tracking, nothing that identifies you. The only time Awning connects to the internet is to check for updates, and only if you turn that on or choose Check for Updates yourself.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no trial, no licence key and no paid tier. Every copy does everything.

Where is Awning after I open it?

In the menu bar at the top right of your screen, not in the Dock. Click its icon to open the sliders.

The colours don't change on macOS 26. Why?

A bug in macOS 26 makes some Macs ignore the colour tables that Awning and similar apps use. Turning off automatic brightness in System Settings, then Displays, helps on many of them. On an affected Mac, flicker-free mode and dimming below 30% still work.

Can I use it with Night Shift or f.lux?

Use one at a time. They change the same colours, so running two at once gives unpredictable results. Turn Night Shift off while Awning is on.

Does it work with external monitors?

Warmth and dimming work on every connected display. Flicker-free mode needs an Apple display, or a monitor that supports DDC/CI with the experimental setting turned on.

What happens when I quit?

Your screen goes straight back to its normal colours, and so does the backlight. If Awning ever crashes, opening it again restores the colours.
